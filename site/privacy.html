Privacy policy

Last updated 31 October 2026.

Bento is a recipe, dinner-plan, shopping-list and pantry app made by an individual developer (Xelvanis on GitHub). This page explains what happens to your information when you use it.

The short version

Bento doesn't send your information to the developer or to anyone else. There are no ads, no analytics and no tracking. Everything you add is stored on the Bento server you choose to connect to, which you or someone you know runs.

What Bento stores, and where

When you use Bento, it stores what you put into it: your account (a username, password, display name and optionally an email address, profile picture and colour), recipes and their photos, ratings, notes, the dinner plan, the shopping list and the pantry.

All of this is stored on the Bento server you log in to. That server is run by you or by the person who invited you (for example on a computer or Raspberry Pi in their home), not by the developer. The people with an account on the same server can see the household's recipes, lists and plans, as sharing them is what Bento is for. The person running the server can see everything stored on it.

The app also keeps a copy of recipes you've opened on your phone, so you can read them without a connection, and remembers your settings and login there. Logging out removes that copy.

What Bento connects to

  • Your Bento server, to load and save everything above.
  • Recipe websites you choose. When you save a recipe from a link, your Bento server fetches that page to read the recipe from it, and the photo from it. When you open a site, a store's page or a search from Bento, it opens in a browser and that website's own privacy policy applies.

Bento doesn't contact the developer, and doesn't use any advertising or analytics services.

Permissions

Bento uses your internet connection to reach your Bento server. When you add a photo to a recipe or your profile, or a saved web page to import, you pick the file yourself; Bento only gets the file you choose.

Deleting your information

You can delete recipes, notes, lists and your photos in the app at any time. To delete your account and everything on it, ask the person who runs your Bento server, or delete the server's data if it's yours. Uninstalling the app removes everything it kept on your phone.

Children

Bento is a household app for adults. It isn't made for children and doesn't knowingly collect their information.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted on this page with a new date.

Contact

Questions about this policy: open an issue at github.com/Xelvanis/bento/issues.
